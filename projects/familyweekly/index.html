The design problem

Join the family conversation without requiring another screen.

Family updates often live inside fast group chats. An older adult who does not comfortably use those channels can miss the everyday stories that create a sense of participation.

The design challenge was not simply to print photos. It was to preserve consent, family separation, readable typography, human approval, and an optional path for the recipient to reply.

The weekly loop

A manual-first workflow with a human decision at every sensitive step.

  1. 01

    Set preferences

    Record the recipient, curator, language, text size, delivery method, and family boundaries.

  2. 02

    Contribute intentionally

    Review up to four photo-caption items and confirm contributor and guardian permissions.

  3. 03

    Build and approve

    Edit captions, arrange stories, preview the complete issue, and invalidate approval after any change.

  4. 04

    Print and deliver

    Produce a two-page large-text newspaper and record only the delivery status a person confirms.

  5. 05

    Reply, optionally

    Capture exact wording and named recipients only when the older adult chooses to share a reply.

  6. 06

    Begin the next issue

    Carry forward only reply text explicitly approved for inclusion, then clear the previous issue.

Responsible design

Privacy is a workflow, not a promise hidden in small print.

Synthetic by default

The Stage 0 experience uses invented people and a bundled synthetic contact sheet. Refreshing discards all work.

Families stay separate

Each workspace keeps its recipient, contributions, approval state, delivery record, and replies isolated.

AI stays assistive

Caption suggestions are predefined and text-only. A human chooses the stories, checks meaning, and approves every issue.

Prototype status

What Stage 0 proves

The working prototype rehearses the complete loop using three fictional family workspaces: contribution review, permission checks, caption editing, layout, curator approval, browser printing, delivery status, reply capture, and reset.

Before real family data can be used, the next stage requires approved hosting and access control, secure family-specific links, consent and withdrawal operations, encrypted storage, retention controls, and a separate privacy and security review.
