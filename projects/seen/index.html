Seen

Put your experience into words.

Seen helps you describe what has been happening, how it affects everyday life, and what you want to ask at an appointment.

Seen organizes what you enter. It does not diagnose, interpret your answers, or recommend treatment.

The design problem

Make a difficult conversation easier to begin.

Period pain can be difficult to summarize during an appointment. Details about timing, daily-life impact, and previous actions may be remembered separately or left out.

Seen provides structure without assigning meaning. The user controls every answer, and the final brief preserves their language rather than rewriting it.

How the concept narrowed

The most responsible feature was deciding what Seen should not do.

01 · Frame the need

Support the conversation.

The design focuses on recalling timing, impact, prior actions, and questions that can otherwise be difficult to assemble during a short appointment.

02 · Protect user meaning

Organize, never interpret.

The interface preserves exact wording and keeps skipped, unanswered, and prefer-not-to-answer states distinct instead of turning missing information into a conclusion.

03 · Bound the prototype

Remove diagnosis and advice.

Fixed prompts, tab-only data, and an appointment brief create a testable communication tool without risk scoring, treatment suggestions, or generated medical guidance.

The experience

Four reflection steps, then review and a user-controlled brief.

  1. Entry

    Introduction

    Explains purpose, privacy, limitations, and the five-minute flow before asking anything sensitive.

  2. Step 1 of 4

    Experience

    Records the user’s description and timing without interpreting or labeling it.

  3. Step 2 of 4

    Impact

    Lets users select affected areas first, then reveals only the relevant impact controls.

  4. Step 3 of 4

    Previous actions

    Documents what the user already tried and what they noticed, without suggesting an action.

  5. Step 4 of 4

    Questions

    Groups a fixed question library under Understanding, Next steps, and Support, with optional notes.

  6. Review

    Check your answers

    Shows exact wording and keeps Not answered, Skipped, and Prefer not to answer meaningfully distinct.

  7. Output

    Appointment brief

    Creates an accessible page the user can print or save as a PDF through the browser.

Responsible design

Privacy and uncertainty are part of the interface.

Tab-only data

Answers remain in JavaScript memory for the active tab. There are no accounts, analytics, external APIs, or browser storage.

Missing is not “no”

Skipped and unanswered fields remain visible as such. The interface never converts absence into a medical statement.

No generated advice

V1 uses fixed prompts and questions so content can be traced, reviewed, and tested before release.

What comes next

What still needs validation

Health copy and the appointment-question library are connected to an authoritative source registry, but they have not been reviewed by a licensed adolescent-health or gynecology professional.

Next testing should include adolescents, trusted adults, clinicians, keyboard and screen-reader users, and real appointment and print workflows.

The prototype is educational communication support. It is not a replacement for a healthcare professional.
